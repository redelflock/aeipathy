The Azari Mahnheeg are responsible for the day to day lives and administration of the city province of Israel. It is within this province that their skills as diplomats and politicians are most greatly required. Closest to the military power that is K'm't and host to a Taengean unit of Hellenic soldiers, the people of Israel are the blockade between war and the peace of Phoenicia. It is up to the Azari family to ensure that conflict is not brought down upon their neighbours. While previous generations have done well in keeping the hostility at bay and the current leader of the house, Gurion, has been upheld as a great peacemaker, the tragedies in the history of the Azari family have produced a more recent generation of angry men who may be destined to upset the delicate balance within the walls of this bright city, throwing its calm and diplomatic future into question. As Israel sits on the western coastline of Phoenicia, it handles much of the nation's maritime trade and is known to be bustling hub of activity throughout the year. Standards of Practice
PoliticsPolitically, the Azari are a little more flexible than other cities. Whilst they would never be considered radical, they are willing to bend or compromise where they think it best for the future of their city. This includes making agreements with the Hellenes military forces or renegotiating trading taxes at the port. In terms of ruling over their own people, the Azari are known to be firm but fair and good listeners.
TradeWhen it comes to trade, Azari have a large coastal region alongside the city. They manage trade from across the Mediterranean and northern Africa. They are required to be a little more open to foreigners, understanding phrases in their language and negotiating in ways that bring profit not conflict.
MilitaryThere is no Phoenician military force in Israel but in order to protect themselves against the kemetic armies to the south, Azari have (for the last few decades) kept a lucrative arrangement with the Hellenic kingdom of Taengea: they supply an encampment of soldiers to the south of the city with men and equipment, and Israel gives them favourable trading reliefs and tax levies at the port. This is not the most popular decision across the Six Cities but it has, thus far, been successful in detering K'm't from entering into open conflict.
ReligionReligiously, the Azari are not the strictest of families but they are very devout. They follow ritual and festivities as dictated in scripture and focus on emulating the intention of the written word: the caring of one's neighbour and being charitable to those less fortunate.

A Mahnheeg desperately trying to maintain its power for future generations but struggling due to the entire clan currently being female. The women of the Barak family may not have been born with great political cunning but are rapidly learning their ways. Led by the sister-wives Zevah and Yonita, the Baraks each have their own skills in intellect gathering and the manipulation of key male officials within the capital of Judah that they use in an effort to keep their position as a Mahnheeg in place. The least stringent of the six Mahnheegs in their faith, the Barak women choose to read the holy scripture in the meaning of - "leave the world a better place than you found it, for the sake of your children". To this end, they use as many means as they can to ensure that they will have a legacy to pass down to their next generation that will - with hope - hold male heirs. Standards of Practice
PoliticsThe Barak family face a political challenge. With the loss of their patriarch, two sister-wives have stepped up to run the capital city. This is not a popular decision but it has proven to be a successful one. The women have maintained the smooth running of the city even as they have to fight to be heard and taken seriously in discussions with other Councilmen. They are known to follow the law of the land with great care but also temper the harsher punishments of doctrine with mercy.
TradeAs leaders of the capital city, Barak have access to many trading opportunities and make a fair profit from the merchants doing business within their walls. Many try to find loopholes and take advantage of the female leadership of the city but both women have a sharp eye for numbers and are quick to bring an end to such plans.
MilitaryThere is no military in Judah and Barak has no intention or plans to change that.
ReligionReligion is a curious influence in the Barak household. Whilst their family is expected to maintain the message, rules and expectations of scripture, the word of Yahweh is very often the antithesis of women in power. That being said, Barak work very hard to follow to the letter of the faith in all other areas, if only to diminish the regular complaints over their seat of power.

The city province of Ammun is home to the largest of markets and trades between Phoenicia and other realms to the north and east; particularly Anatolia. With a few arabian-looking faces on the streets and products and goods from all over Asia and Europe being peddled at its markets, the family who lead the settlement have to be tolerant to a certain point. Focusing on the money and profit that can be accomplished from these strangers and how the taxes of such trades then go towards Phoenicia as a whole and the service of Yahweh, allows the Haviv family to continue building their flourishing economy, regardless of who they are making deals with. Unlike in Moab where a favour is worth a thousand gold pieces, the Phoenicians of Ammun work exclusively in coin and refuse to take payment in anything else. They are skilled crafters and know the worth of an item at immediate glance. All appraisers by nature, it is the Haviv family's responsibility to ensure that no disagreements or fights break out within this competitive atmosphere and to handle the aftermath of any raids or thefts that are a frequent challenge upon their borders. The people of Ammun are loud and boisterous and all about their goods and trade which means the Haviv must have strong personalities, if they are to lead a people comprised of such firm wills. The family is one of the richest in Phoenicia and enjoys luxuries that others might consider to be slightly materialistic but they are careful to never step too far into false idolism or shallow thinking. Standards of Practice
PoliticsHaviv rule over Ammun which has a very large populace. The only fair way to deal with so many is to keep hard and true to the rules and regulations of the city. This is a rigid and inflexible method of rule that doesn't really allow for extraneous circumstances or mitigating factors. But it works well on a grand scale.
TradeAs a massive trading point for travellers from the east and north, Ammun has many influences from all over. They are also fairly good at communicating (at least in basic phrases) with foreigners and negotiating fair trades and prices. The Haviv's main income comes from this massive influx of trade.
MilitaryThere is no military in Ammun and Haviv has no intention or plans to change that.
ReligionHaviv are as devout as any other leading family. Though certain members may balk a little at the gender norms expected of scripture, they conform publicly and lead the city as good, faithful examples of religious practice.

The Jaffe family refuse to leave Damascus for most any reason, barring the monthly meet of the Council of Elders. And, even then, they often only send the Mahnheeg or his deputy councilman - rarely both. A secluded people in the spine of mountains that spear through the Damascus desert plains, the citizens of Damascus like the peace and quiet that their life affords them. Due to their piousness and their faith, many within Damascus are harsh in their judgments of those who follow different paths and are not known for their welcoming presence. Instead, the smallest piece of gossip is reacted to with the greatest of scandalised expressions and the merchants who deliver the goods the city needs to survive each month are treated with suspicion and slanted gazes. The city itself is known as a site of pilgrimage for the scholarly and the devout with massive libraries and archival rooms of history and scripture. These sacred walls do not bring in profit or economic security but the Six Cities support its maintenance due to a deep belief in its posterity and value. The Jaffe family are known for their scholarly and austere ways and they lead their city province with a quiet but iron hand. They are believed to be incredibly fair-minded as their discipline and dignified exterior project competence and wisdom. Standards of Practice
PoliticsThe Jaffe are the very definition of rigid. They follow faith and law down to the letter without consideration, mercy or tempering. They are equally feared and respected for their stringent discipline and loyalty to detail.
TradeNot a lot of trade goes through the Damascus mountains. Visitors that do make the journey usually do so specifically to trade with Jaffe and supply the city and its people with their necessities. There is limited space and means for natural growth and sufficiency farming in Damascus, so most daily needs are brought to them. This is a cost the Six Cities jointly swallow as Damascus is seen as hub of faith and history - worth keeping populated and cared for, even if they operate at a trading loss.
MilitaryThere is no military in Damascus and Jaffe has no intention or plans to change that.
ReligionWhen it comes to religion, doctrine and scripture, the Jaffe family are the ultimate authority. They spend most of their time and efforts studying, preserving and interpreting the details of the faith and of the world at large. Their libraries and archives are immense and many make pilgrimage to Damascus to study under their tutorship and view the sacred documents.

Leading the great city of Jerusalem, there is no surprise or doubt that the Meier family is most devout. Believing the power of the great God Almighty - specifically his ability to smite the wicked and bring hell to the damned - the Meier Mahnheeg lead their people (they believe) with the backing of divine provenance. To go against the word of the Mahnheeg is to go against Yahweh. Jerusalem is a place of faith and worship but it is also a space of little mercy and even less forgiveness. The Meier family are firm in their convictions and ensure that the people follow them to the same highest standards, with a guard of city protectors patrolling the streets to ensure sinless behaviour on the part of their flock. Standards of Practice
PoliticsMeier like to take the harsher and more judgmental side of the faith and put it into even harsher practice. If Damascus is the hub of all historical faith, Jerusalem is the present, active representation of it. And the Meier take their stewardship of it very seriously. As if to prove themselves worth of the position, they emphasise their rulership with harsh legal doctrine and almost cruel judgment, carried out in the name of fairness and justice.
TradeThough Jerusalem isn't a merchantile mecca, it is a very large city which naturally produces a lot of trade and business. The Meier family take their share of this as it's leading family and arranges and organises the tax system. This brings them moderate wealth that they tend to funnel back into the city.
MilitaryThough there is no formal military in Jerusalem, the Meier family have funded and organised The City Protectors: young men armed with long knives and expected to keep the peace as a rudimentary police force.
ReligionReligiously, Meier are so tight to scripture it makes your eyes water. They like to take a literal and highly conservative reading of the word of Yahweh and are heavy on gender norms, ritual expectations and practices of faith. They are incredibly devout and hold themselves to these strict ordinances just as they do everyone else.

The Poraz family is one of great spirit. Ruling Moab with a hand as gentle as it is firm, the family are known for their wisdom, their understanding and their tolerance of others. Being a city province at the centre of trade for most of Phoenicia, Moab and its ruling Mahnheeg are those with the least frozen identity of their own; fluid and changing as those who do business within their walls. The people of the city province of Moab are barterers, hagglers and traders - looking for the next best deal and trustworthy merchants or financiers to do business with. The word and favour of a man is highly valued and sometimes money is not the true purpose of an agreement. The Moab Phoenicians believe in the sanctity of a promise made and the Poraz family are the epitome of this social grace, dependability and understanding. Standards of Practice
PoliticsThe city of Moab is a bustling one and that can make keeping the law and its policies straight, a challenge. Whilst the Poraz family believe in the importance of order and legality, they are also realists. Sometimes, rules must be broken and policies overruled if you are to get through the day without issue. In this way, Poraz are known to be the most flexible and fluid of leaderships; weak in the eyes of some and clever to others.
TradeTrade is one of Moab's biggest industries. Mostly from the eastern regions beyond the map of Aeipathy and into the persian and middle eastern caravans. They are also one of the largest traders within Phoenicia, using the Dead Sea as a trading highway between the Six Cities.
