A decade ago, the Asmar family was burned to the ground. Literally. With the family's economics founded on smiths and forges, the majority of their wealth was reduced to ash in a blaze that also took the lives of the patriarch and his wife. For the last decade, the new head of the family has worked day and night just to reach solvency, stretching his pockets and his morals too thinly. His efforts have clawed a level of respect in political circles, focusing on the family's resilience and dignity in the face of trauma, even as meager finances and resources continue to limit their power. The shadow of smoke still lingers over the Asmar name, however, as the fire that once destroyed everything was no accident. The survivors know it, and the upper echelons of society know it. Yet no clues or confessions have come forth. The Asmar maintain a polite and respectful connection with their peers but privately atune their gaze with mistrust and suspicion, waiting for the day when the arsonist is revealed and their revenge can be wrought. Standards of Practice
PoliticsThe Asmar family does not have the power nor financial backing to have a strong influence on politics. Whilst they hold neither animosity nor great honour in the upper echelons of society, they do hold a position of basic respect amongst the Council of Angals.
TradeThe province ruled by Asmar does not have strong economic and trade ties. Their largest export are wool and products from the sheep and goats reared on their slopes. The family live frugally and often struggle to make ends meet.
MilitaryThe Asmar run a small military unit as personal guards and security for their province but do not have a formal army or private militia. They are not involved in the privatised military hierarchy of Assyria. Their province is challenging to attack and its people have learned to protect the valleys and crevices of their home. But otherwise, the Asmar have little to no connection with military forces.
ReligionAs their brother tries to keep the family afloat, the Asmar sisters are its strongest source of respect. Both are heavily involved in the temples and religious rites of the land. They are openly admired for their efforts and occasionally treated with more respect than their brother. Izla in particularly within religious circles and Issavi within courtly circles.

TyarehThere is little to no connection - positive or negative - between Asmar and Tyareh.
Yal'iraThe sisters of Asmar have strong connections with the sisters of Yal'ira. Both are deeply involved in the religious spheres of upper society. There is an imbalance in power, however, as Asmar greatly relies on Yal'ira's trading hubs for selling its exports and buying in food resources.

INTRO Standards of Practice
PoliticsAs the reigning regnal House of Assyria, Kushiq are the most heavily involved in politics and the ruling of the country. They are responsible for the welfare of the entire nation and take that duty very seriously.
TradeAs the rulers of the nation, Kushiq have a significant role in the royal treasury, taxes and trading regulations across the land. They have monumental wealth of their own and rule over provinces with heavy trade and commerce.
MilitaryKushiq's private army is one of the largest in the nation. This makes them extremely powerful in military spheres. Particularly as their trading power can fund massive war campaigns where necessary.
ReligionThough not passionately involved in religious circles, the princesses of Kushiq dutifully perform their responsibilities in the temples and the family lead many religious festivals and ceremonies as part of their royal duties.
