For the last four generations, the House of Ammuna has steadily grown in respect and repute. Never one for scandal, Ammuna has recently been led by a series of matriarchs, including Faemya Ammuna, grandmother to the current generation and a beloved pseudo-aunt to the king. In exchange for her doting, the Tabarna now has a soft spot for Faemya's descendants and has supported their gradual rise to glory. In recent months, however, a social faux pas has led to unavoidable punishment. Taken from the a prestigious seat in Alinna, the household has been relocated to Carchemish, a challenging and isolated posting that many see as a set up for failure. The more cunning of the Hittite Court, however, wonder if this is a more subtle use of the king's favouritism. The fortress city in the east may be a toiling one. But to succeed under such circumstances would catapult Ammuna cleanly over their recent transgressions. The only question is, will the young leadership of Ammuna sink or swim? Standards of Practice
PoliticsAmmuna does not actively partake in ambitious politics. They have always believed that loyalty to the crown and diligently pursuing one's responsibilities will eventually yield the political position one deserves. Genuine, personal friendships with the ruling house, however, may be seen by some as undue or worrisome influence.
TradeThe Ammuna has skilled accountants and managers of trade but not proactive wheelers and dealers. Their business has always been primarily with the crown, making it more about duty than profit. So, whilst they can handle trading and the processes of commerce, they are not generally thought of as merchants.
MilitaryThe current leader of Ammuna has experience in combat but this is not the household's forte. They have, however, always been strongly connected to be the industry of the military. Previously leaders of the ironworks in Alinna, it was always the duty of Ammuna to ensure the production of blades and essential equipment for soldiers. Now, they rule over the great fortress city of Carchemish in the East - an entirely different cornerstone of Hattusa's military power.
ReligionRespect towards the gods is a necessity of life but not the axis around which the Ammuna house operates. As the current generation is predominantly female and working in the temples is an acceptable role for ladies of the court, Ammuna has a reasonable hand in the practicalities of faith, even if they themselves are not grand authorities on the subject.

For the Hantili, one's mind and morals are a greater measure of worth than wealth. Which is lucky as they have little of the latter. Unfortunately, they are also struggling to retain the former. A household that has gradually fallen into disrepair and onto frugal means, Hantili has suffered still further in its honour than its coin purse. After scandalous rumours decimated the respect of their parents' generation, the current household must piece together what little dignity remains. This burden falls heavily on their leader, who will need to find exorbitant doweries for his two unwed cousins, if they are to be considered marriage material. But how is one to build riches when no-one will do business with a tainted house? In the meantime, Hantili's greatest strength is in their education. Though their reputation lies in tatters, it is hard to deny the brilliance of Turah Tepeniya and his sister-cousins in the Council of Ministers. Still, the occasional approving nod may not be enough to keep this noble house afloat... Standards of Practice
PoliticsPolitically, it is all Hantili can do to stay afloat. After complete social disagrace in the previous generation, this little house of three must band together and perform to a standard that is above reproach. And even then they will only be treated with the barest civility. Luckily, their impressive education, advanced political knowledge and thoughtful wisdom does occasionally bring them a little spark of recognition in powerful circles.
TradeAs leaders of Kizzuwatna - one of the largest trading ports in Hattusa - the Hantili's main business is in trade. For the last eight years, however, the focus has been on rebuilding a trusting network of business partners, not high profits. Their wealth is limited and their status as merchants lowly.
MilitaryThe Hantili have never had an interest in the military. It is not their skillset. Their education, however, has afforded them a decent knowledge of military tactics and international diplomacy.
ReligionIn terms of religious practice, the Hantili tick all the boxes of devout behaviour but in the hopes that further misfortune will not come their way, than an actual desire to worship.

His cut-throat policies and iron-fisted determination has served the house well in both fields. Now a powerful voice in the Hittite Council, Suppilu are drawing suspicious glances. PoliticsThe political influence Suppily holds is an enigmatic one. On one hand, their leader is clearly a competitive and ambitious man who demands success in all areas of his life. The house of Tahurna has held the throne of Hattusa from its very conception, two hundreds years ago. And, with a plethora of male heirs and grandchildren, it seems unlikely to lose the crown any time soon. A life of ease and luxury, however, is antithesis to this royal family. A mentality of service permeates from one generation to the next, reminding princes and princesses alike that to rule is a privilege and to a position of caretaking; that they must always be proven worthy of such an honour. Such pressure, combined with the complex social politics of such a vast family and steep competition for power, is a persistent test on the foundations of this household, forging some and crippling others. And all of it on display for the Court and the public. Standards of Practice
PoliticsAs the ruling house, the Tahurna are heavily involved in all matters of politics at the highest levels. This has been educated to its heirs and descendants, making even the most impatient of princesses a minor expert on law and diplomacy. The significance of their authority is not lost of Tahurna and they take their responsibilities as leaders of their people very seriously.
TradeThe Tahurna house is not a merchant one but they are deeply involved in the commerce industry of their empire. They rule over the royal treasury, the empire's taxation system and the sponsorship of its council ministers and the necessary funding of its provinces.
MilitaryThe second area in which Tahurna place a lot of significance is the military. With the Gal Meshedi (Commander in Chief) always selected from the royal family and the monarch often an active leader of his soldiers on the front lines, the men of this house are almost always combat trained and excessively so. If one is to hold the benefits of a royal life, they must be willing to lay that life on the line for their people.
ReligionAs the royal house, the members of the Tahurna family are often required to lead the formal, national ceremonies for the temples. To that end, most women in the house become priestesses and serve in the temples of the capital to ensure that they are experts in needs and rituals of each god. A true leader, after all, ensures their people are protected both from mortal enemies but the immortal wrath of offended deities.

Tudanta may be one of Hattusa's newest houses but holds the rare and prestigious claim of close royal blood. Whilst all noble houses of Hattusa were once descendants of the Tahurna bloodline, the matriarch of Tudanta is cousin to the king. These close connections elevate Tudanta beyond its shallow roots and into the highest circles of authority. Whilst Hepeliya is a practiced and beloved socialite, her latest husband is a divisive figure from a foreign land and a poison at the heart of her household. For Hepeliya is unaware that her first husband perished at the hands of her second. While the house of Tudanta stands as a beacon of morality and etiquette across the Hittite Court, behind closed doors thse awkward familial bonds are beginning to fray... Standards of Practice
PoliticsTudanta has the closest family ties to the current monarch. This gives them immense political clout around the king and at Court meetings. Though this power comes through their matriarch, it is her husband that actively chases political power and influence in the Council of Ministers. As leaders of Arzawa - one of the busiest trading ports in the land - they also have the financial means for back-dealings and social manipulation.
TradeThe heir of Tudanta rules over the port city of Arzawa. Barely out of his teen years, it is his father who truly handles the reins. Next to Kizzuwatna, Arzawa is one of the largest and most profitable trading hubs, connecting Hattusa to the western world. The taxes alone from such business are immense. This level of responsibility and financial authority are being wielded with cunning efficiency by Tudanta's patriarch, affording him a growing powerbase in Hittite Council.
MilitaryThe military is of little interest to the Tudanta household who focus on the might of money over the power of a sword.
ReligionThe Tudanta ladies are a devout trio who are heavily involved in the rituals of the capital's temples. They take the teachings of the gods very seriously and are the leading authority on the religious matters of state.

Tragedy once razed the Zuziya family to the ground and placed young and inexperienced siblings a the helm of one of the wealthiest houses in the land. Thankfully, the pair have risen to the occasion. Each year of profit has brought a sense of security and trust to the Zuziya name and they now sustain their influential position at Court based, not solely on legacy, but upon their own accomplishments. Many whisper that Ilbanu Nanuun will eventually become a leading figure in the Council of Ministers and few see the emotional turmoil and traumatic grief still festering at the heart of this house. Standards of Practice
PoliticsAs one of the wealthiest houses in Hattusa with a monopoly on stone and the necessities of the empire's infrastructure, Zuziya have a colossal influence in the Council of Ministers. Or at least the doting respect of its members. Zuziya do not have an active greed for political power - they merely hold it as a natural benefit of their merchantile business.
TradeTrade is the Zuziya bread, butter and stone. It is their legacy and their inheritance. After the decimation of their household, the family treasury has become a copying mechanism and its continuous growth an obsessive necessity. The only drive equal to this is the caretaking Nurek and its people, which the house take very seriously.
MilitaryWhilst their local militia is well funded and cared for, the Zuziya house does not take a personal interest or investment in the military.
ReligionZuziya are not a particularly faithful family but their province is heavily influenced by the worship of Teshub and Sarruma in particular. To that end, they must fulfil many ceremonies and rituals in their honour. Personally, however, the remaining descendants of Zuziya struggle with the association of divine processions and the loss of their family.
