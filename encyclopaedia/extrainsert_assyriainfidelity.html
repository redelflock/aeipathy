Royalty The monarch of the land keeps a seraglio of female concubines. He is the only man in the nation able to do so. These concubines do not count as second wives and are more servants with an elevated status. They share the lugal's bed and their children have the potential to be royal heirs if no legitimate males are born. They are often kept in luxury but can be turned out at any moment if they displease their lugal. A monarch is legally entitled to bring any female into his seraglio so long as she is unmarried and he has her father's consent. Widows are also allowed to be claimed. Sleeping with any woman in the seraglio is not considered cheating on the legal queen. Polygamy / Concubines Men of all classes are permitted legally permitted multiple wives in Assyria. As wives are an added expense to the household, as are any children they produce, and women are less likely to work or provide a second income to the family, this is practice more common among the wealthy and upper classes. Men of this status will quite often have two or three wives without issue. Lower classes may be able to afford two. But for the common people a single spouse is the norm, simply due to practicalities. Sleeping with any woman who is not your legal wife is considered adultery. It is an offence that can have a man brought up on charges and paying a fine. But it is grounds for divorce.

The first marriage of a man always holds great significance. This woman will become their first wife and hold certain responsibilities and rights that any future wives do not. The title of first wife cannot be transferred or removed from this woman. Men must therefore choose their first partner with care. This might mean choosing a woman they truly love or, more often, a woman who is a suitable partner or subservient spouse (depending on their needs). The second and third wives are more often the women that hold a man'semotional affection or physical desire. Multiple-Partners It is illegal for women to take multiple husbands in Assyria. It is also socially unacceptable for women to be involved in group sexual activities. A husband will never go to bed with both of his wives at the same time. Sexual congress is for two and two alone.

Any wife who is found to have sexual contact with another woman has committed adultery. Any wife who has had physical contact of any sort with any man other than her husband or a blood-relative (i.e. their father or brother), has committed adultery. Punishment for this crime varies depending on the contact. A woman who accidentally brushes the hand of their husband's male guest when serving tea has technically been unfaithful. Their husband might have serious words to say about it behind closed doors and the guest would be supremely awkward. However, it would likely be treated as a very serious (but passing) faux pas. Even though, legally, it is grounds for a divorce and charges brought upon the wife. Actively passionate contact - even something as simple as a deliberate holding of the hand - is, therefore, a huge deal and women will often lose their marriages, social status and means of living for so small an act.

Men at War Unlike in other lands where war campaigns can operate as a sort of 'hall pass' for extramarital affairs, soldiers are expected to remain just as loyal to their wives when away as they are at home. As punishment for male adultery is not particularly harsh, though, many at war will take the fine on the chin and do what they wish. However, as the mentality is to always be loyal to one's spouse, it is likely that there are more faithful husbands in the Syrian army than in other lands, where promiscuity in wartime is more accepted. Brothels and Sex Workers Despite women being unable to give consent for sexual conduct before marriage, sex workers and brothels do still exist in Assyria. But they are hidden and carefully arranged so that they are never publicly admitting to their purpose. They are known as specialised tea rooms or dance houses. Dancing girls and serving ladies will tend to men in private rooms. But it is widely known by both the patrons and the performers that sex is an expected part of the arrangement. So long as men who are caught frequenting such establishments have a legal (if completely transparent) excuse for their visit.

Attending a dancing house like this would be seen as adultery and disrespectful to one's wife. A man who is caught in the act of such infidelity will be charged accordingly. The woman he was with will likely become the scapegoat for the business as a whole and turned out. Any establishment that cannot place the blame on the individuals involved would be shut down.

Women are not permitted access to dancing halls and establishements like brothels. As women are barred from several places at different times of day, this is not an obvious sign that a business is a brothel. But it does mean that women will never have the opportunity to pay for sex. Even if they wished to from another woman.

Businesses of this sort that offer men (for female patrons) do not exist. Business that offer men (for male patrons) do exist but are even harder to find that the hetero-catering ones. They are few and far between, found only on personal recommendation and are often located in unseemly parts of town. Curiously, these gay brothels are more likely to avoid legal penalisation if their patrons are unmarried. Unlike with female performerrs, who can give no legal consent, males don't have this issue. So long as all performers and customers are male, unmarried and above the age of consent, there's actually nothing illegal about what they are doing. Even though, socially, this sort of place would be considered a den of disgusting perversion.
