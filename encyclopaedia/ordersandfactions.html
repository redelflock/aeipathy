A military group like no other, the Athenian Guard is a unit of soldiers sworn to the monarch, the city and each other. These men are tasked with the protection of the city they live and serve in whether that be manning the city walls in case of an attack or simply clearing the streets of drunkards after a rowdy night. They are the ones who keep the peace and ensure that all are secure under their protective vigil, day and night. The Athenian Guards are the chief policing force in the kingdom and in addition to running the city’s fylaki, it is not uncommon to see men from its ranks patrolling the streets with their signature blue sashes to mark them as a member of this order.

Although the protection of the capital city is their chief priority, the Athenian Guard is especially noteworthy for their dedication to the crown and whoever wears it. They will protect the monarch at any cost but they will not involve themselves in the petty disputes between the Dynasteías as to who should be wearing it. After all, they are above the messy business of politics and are under the firm belief that it will bring nothing but chaos into their ranks. That being said, noblemen are not required to forgo any titles in order to join the Guard’s ranks. They are simply expected to remain silent on any family loyalties whilst on active duty. If they cannot do so, they will be sent home in disgrace.

Valuing a man’s talents over any other quality, the Athenian Guard only allows those of an adequate skill level into their active ranks. However, they allow any citizen of the city, noble-born or common, into its barracks and are one of the few units in Hellas willing to spend years training new recruits until they reach said level of expertise. This high standard and dedication to filling their ranks with skilled fighters is what distinguishes them from other, similar orders and encourages a level of camaraderie between the men and respect between the citizens and the Guard. In exchange, though, all those within the unit are expected to dedicate their lives to their service and retiring from the guard is rare before the grip of old age. Standards of Practice

History
The origins of the Athenian Guard can be found several hundred years ago, back before Athenia was the shining gem that it is today. Back then, the young city had grown faster than it could support and mass numbers with limited resources produced desperate and criminal acts. For years, noblemen blamed the incivility of the lower classes for the violence on the streets. Those of common birth blamed a nobility failing to care for its people. Tensions came to a head when Nicon of Anagnostou, the eldest son of a baron, was found dead in the street.

It was clear that his passing had not been peaceful by any means. Shards of terracotta were scattered on the ground around him and left in gaping wounds. To this day, his death is an unsolved mystery. However, the true horror came after his death and before he was found. The dogs and rats had scavenged the body whilst lowlifes of the city had stripped the corpse of anything of value, including the royal blue himation that the man had been adorned with. Dirt upon the body was evidence of several of the thieves literally stepping upon the dead in their desperate, heartless need for his possessions. The sheer inhumanity of the death affected men and women at all levels of society. Whoever did this had to be brought to justice.

The king himself offered a handsome reward for whoever found the responsible party. With the promise of gold shining in the eyes of desperate men, many flocked to the palace gates with raised voices proclaiming that they knew who had done the terrible deed, but none could provide proof so the prize went unclaimed. The common people began to feel frustrated; such attention to a nobleman's death when similar fates befell their loved ones any other day of the week was distasteful. Members of the Anagnostou family were met with protests and angry mobs demanding similar reparation for their own dead. When the last of Anagnostou's male heirs were killed in a chariot accident, spurred by a frustrated crowd, something had to be done.

Nobles gathered with demands to their king for a military unit; one that would ensure their safety on the streets. Though hesitant to introduce more weaponry to the skirmishes in the city, the king accepted that matters could not continue as they were. He appointed the House of Antonis with the task of setting up a fresh military unit for the capital itself. Within a week, the military experts of Antonis had taken Lieutenants and promising young fighters from their own province militias and brought together nearly fifty men to serve in the capital's first policing force.

As the sun set over the city, this new brigade was dispersed into the streets in small groups of heavily armoured men all under the orders to put a stop to any deviance they came across. There was only one rule: they were not to kill anyone, regardless of the crime committed. Drunkards were escorted to their homes, brawlers were incapacitated on the spot and any thief to be found was immediately hauled to makeshift jails throughout the city to await punishment for their crime. Initially the action was a failure; the soldiers quickly became targets for the frustration of the people. But the Antonis were harsh on their men and unyielding in their rules. Eventually, the soldiers who would not kill or maim but contained and supported those from all backgrounds began to turn public opinion. Within a month, the nobles had criticisms of the Guard for not operating in favour of the upper classes. But the common people saw the Guard as heroes.

At the king's words, the nobles were silenced and the Guard continued in their practice of equal treatment. Commoner and noblefolk alike grew used to the relief of city streets now safe to walk at night. Of course, such progression did not eradicate crime entirely. And the Guard was soon outfitted with equipment and a recognisable uniform: a sash and cloak combination, in the same bright blue shreds of Nicon of Anagnostou's himation. The Anagnostou family might have fallen out of existence but their legacy would continue. The king eventually agreed to a special fund to support the Guard and one of the old estate houses, now fallen out of use, was converted to a city barracks for the men. This facility included places for rest and training, but also armament stores and a city prison for holding drunkards and arrested criminals.

Currently, the Athenia Guard work on rotation, protecting the city walls, the docklands and the Inner Circle, as well as organising patrols throughout the city day and night. They also supply the Royal Palace's guards which are maintained under a specialist Captain who reports to the Commander of the Guard. These men have their own rotations and shifts that are appropriately matched to the royals they are protecting. Roles and Titles

InitiationJoining the Guard All men are welcomed to join the ranks of the Athenian Guard, regardless of their social rank but they must be prepared to fully commit their lives to the order. New initiates move into the city’s barracks and live the life of a common soldier, completing the laborious menial tasks required by the unit. Some born to high classes can escape this if they're already an officer in a provincial military but even then their skills are put to the test in a monthly arena-style test. All initiatives, regardless of whether they have been so for a single day or ten years, are pitted against one of two highly-skilled soldiers (one noble, one common) selected by the Commander. If they are victorious in two-on-one combat, the recruit is welcomed into the Guard.
The Feast of Men and KingsAn Annual Feast In honour of the agreement made between Guard and Crown to provide mutual support for one another, an annual feast is held within the palace walls for the members of the nobility and a lucky fifty full members of the Guard (selected by random ballot). Hosted in early spring, the courtyard is transformed into three dining areas; the Guard, the nobility and one raised table reserved for the monarch and the Commander of the Guard. Paid for by the nobility, this feast serves as a way to honour the soldiers’ dedication to keeping the peace in a way unexperienced by other military units. It is a fantastic experience to be one of the fifty men selected to join the Commander during the feast and some Guards become quite competitive for the chance to be chosen and mingle with the upper-classes; trading ballot tickets and bribing friends for theirs. Whilst the soldiers of the Guard may have seen certain areas of the palace whilst guarding it, a formal meal and being treated as one of the people they normally shadow is an entirely different opportunity and a highly sought reward.
ElectionsAssigning the Deputy-Commander It is also at the Feast of Men and Kings that the Commander of the Guard will name his deputy to serve by his side for the upcoming year. If the Commander should retire or be otherwise unfit for duty, the Deputy immediately becomes the new leader. However, simply being named Deputy is not enough to ensure that one will ascend to that position. All active and retired Captains of the order must anonymously vote on the Commander's choice for a Deputy by drawing either a Circle or a Square, representing yes and no respectively, on a small piece of parchment which is placed upon the high table with the king and Commander. As these men are casting their votes, any member of the Guard is allowed to rise from their seat and speak on behalf of the nominee. Although it is frowned upon to speak against the man being voted on, no one is prevented from doing so. After all the votes have been cast and the decision is read, if the nominated Deputy has the majority vote, he is welcomed up to the raised table to sit beside the Commander for the remainder of the evening. A Deputy is nominated every year regardless of whether the position is currently filled. More often than not, the Deputy from the previous year is nominated and able to continue serving. Serving for even a year is considered a prestigious honour, so not continuing in the role of Deputy after a year is not a loss or considered a failure: merely the way the system works.
Joining the Order
Any trainees or initiatives that are interested in joining the Guard are required to move into the Guard's barracks, don a simplified version of the Guard's uniform and armour (but with a white sash instead of a blue one) and must perform the menial and maintenance tasks of the barracks. Every month, an Initiation Test day is held and all trainees currently living within the barracks have the choice of taking the exam. On this day, they are required to beat in one-on-one combat an existing member of the Guard. An unsuccessful participant is not rejected from the barracks but, instead, allowed to remain inside the Guard's home, continuing their menial tasks until the next testing day. They can continue to do this as many times as they like or need. There is a certain level of pride and fame in passing the Guard's Initiation Day on the first attempt. However, there is also a level of respect for a man determined to keep trying. A trainee who has failed many times might be met with ridicule and begrudging respect in equal measure. Once the initiate has passed their Testing Day, they offer their oath of loyalty to the Guard and become a fully-fledged member of the Guard, are factored into duty rotation and are permitted to live outside of the barracks. They must, however, live within the city. Passing a Testing Day can be written as a normal thread of your choice. However, if there are multiple members of the Guard in play, it is worth contacting staff in case the Testing Day would work well for a site-wide event.

History
In the early days, the Babylonian military stayed separate from the palace fighting battles against enemy nations on far away borders. Then, during the reign of Lugal Cherib an'Ashina, the borders of Babylonia's great empire drew in closer. War came to the capital city's front gates and the world changed. From then on, Babylonia has existed as a smaller city state and trained its fighters to defend the battlements of their capital as one would the sacred plains of the borderlands.

The original enforcers were a rag-tag unit of rebellious thinkers and maverick fighters. They were inventive on the walls and would orchestrate ambushes and attacks on the Lake Milh and canals north of the city. Their experiences were eventually written down and formalised into training protocols for the docks and riverbanks of the city. And as the walls and battlements around the capital grew, so too did the handbook of those trained to defend them.

Within twenty years Babylon became an impenetrable fortress. But even the thickest walls can allow the slippery entry of rats. Spies and espionage became the only means of breaching the city, making the noble manors and palaces a target for enemy plundering. The Enforcers were eventually summoned to the imperial palace of Lugal Ninospali, son of the late Cherib and a great admirer of the newly formed order. His instructions would later become fact, then evolve into tradition: the Enforcers would be the authority not only one defending the city walls but the corridors and halls of the imperial palace and all government buildings within the city.

For over a hundred years the Babylon Enforcers have kept the streets of the capital safe. They arrest criminals and trouble makers, treasonous spies and enemy collaborators alike. They are so familiar with Babylonian architecture, canals and acqueducts that they blend into the stone, seeming to move around the streets like ghosts. Training was required to ensure these soldiers knew how to move through civilian populations and districts with speed and keep innocent bystanders away from conflict. Eventually, a new institution and regime was formed for specialised training. No soldier could become a qualified Enforcer without completing this additional labour of learning. Roles and Titles

Only when an Erim is a master of the city and its ways and means can they graduate as an Enforcer and choose their preferred sector: Watchmen or Watermen.

It is less about whether an Erim achieves the challenge than how they go about tackling it as many of its challenges focus on unpredictability and how a soldier with innovate and think on their feet; how they apply their training with wisdom. This event is held annually and is a grand celebration in the city with residents and common peerage helping or hindering the contestants. Upon the completion of the event, the City Captain decides who has performed well enough to graduate. Those that have not must try again the next year. At the end of the graduation challenge, all newly confirmed Watchman and Waterman will be marked with a henna tattoo on the back of their neck. It is the Babylonian symbol for "brotherhood". This mark lasts approximately three months. When it fades, the soldier's probation period is over and they are given a silver pendant / token with the same mark as the tattoo. They are expected to wear this whilst on duty but most wear it permanently. Some even seek to have the mark of their order tattooed permanently into their skin or renew the henna mark repeatedly elsewhere on their body (as to add it again to the nape of their neck would mark them as a newcomer instead of a respected veteran).
Brotherhood FestivalAnnual Event Whilst the daytime is dedicated to the Graduation Challenge for all knew members of the order, the evening and night become a festival of celebration. Soldiers that have been lost in conflict over the last year are mourned and their spirits celebrated. Those who have been promoted are saluted and drank to. Those who have graduated are rewarded and those who failed commisserate with their brothers. It is the ultimate punishment for foolishness or misbehaviour to be assigned guard duty during the Brotherhood Festival.

The Babylon Enforcers are selected from the soldiers who graduate from a Babylonian military academy. Soldiers that are chosen by the Enforcers or who apply to the order themselves are carefully selected. No one may join the Enforcers without first completing the academy's training. As Enforcer training is so specific, Erim rarely transfer back from the Enforcers to the normal military after their training and probationary period but may do so during them. History
The order was founded countless generations ago, and nobody outside of the brotherhood agrees on when or how. But tales of the Ghoði tell the Saga thusly: Long ago, there were two brothers, Thorkell the Axe and Hamar the Grim-Eyed. Thorkell was a canny warrior, a breaker of shields and spirits, whilst his brother Hamar was one of the most cunning potion-makers to have ever walked the land.

Battle after battle they fought in, serving lords and masters alike. Thorkell’s strength was magnified by the brews that Hamar made him and he would break many a foeman in rains of blood. When combat had passed, however, Thorkell would need to restrain the beast within; a beast that demanded more violence upon one and all. Witnessing madness in his eyes, Thorkell was shunned by many a Jarl, making the brothers nomads of strength and gore.

Whispers of blasphemy followed them. For neither Thorkell nor Hamar carried Odin’s spear or Thor’s hammer about their neck. Whilst their peers spoke of Ragnarok as an inevitable tragedy of the turning wheel; a moment in which the Gods would fall and the world would be plunged into darkness, Thorkell and Hamar awaited Ragnarok with eagerness. Believing it the simple fate of nature and a necessity for renewal and growth, the brothers saw Fenrir, the great wolf that would begin the ending of all, as a savior of life; the reaper who would strip the world clean and plant new seeds. Thorkell and Hamar never denied the power and privilege of the Aegis deities; they simply saw the tales of the worlds-ending in a different light. A curious, blasphemous, oddly-optimistic light. Some called it treachery to the gods. Others an open acceptance of destiny: that all men must die.

Those who shared in this philosophy began to join the brothers. Those prepared to walk the bloody road of the wild pair were taught the ways of battle by Thorkell and how to channel the power of Hamar's sacred draught. The small band of fighters quickly became a force for destruction that no man could match. Those who did not excel in the physical prowess of the warriors, were taught the creation of the previous elixir by Hamar.

With greater numbers came greater needs. And needs required money. Thorkell and Hamar were soon leaders of a service, lent to konungrs and jarls at precious cost. Additional plunder from their victories further lined the pockets of the fledgling order.

The warband swelled and grew more powerful, those that joined the brothers bore the finest weapons, the strongest shields, the standards of great champions who had fallen beneath their unyielding boots. A home was needed for such resources and for the rested head of men. 'The Berserkers', as they were now named in fearful whispers, journeyed to the lands of the Ironwind and the fresh air of the coast, where the waters teemed with fish. They used their vast wealth to build the fortress of Tiwsborg on a high, defensible crag, and thus the brotherhood was given a home.

History
The City Protectors are a relatively new order established by Simeon of Nissim, who formed them to protect his city from the rising levels of crime that had begun to permeate through the streets as population numbers naturally increased.

In recent years, prior to the order's creation, Jerusalem had been the victim of growing unrest. The city was facing an increase in petty crimes and lawlessness that brazenly challenged the city's sacred principles. With no official military within Phoenicia, miscreants grew bolder and more aggresive: pickpockets roamed the streets, merchants cheated their customers and public brawls became commonplace. Whilst wrong-doers were often caught and hauled before the Mahnheeg offices for punishment by the common people or good samaritan neighbours, this did little to stem the tide. As far as the criminals of Jerusalem were concerned, the city was a free ground for acts of defiance.

The situation finally came to a head when a group of desperate thieves attempted to infiltrate the Holy Sanctuary itself, seeking to steal sacred relics from within its hallowed halls and smuggle them from the city. Although they were caught before any true harm could be done, the very thought of such sacrilege shook the city to its core. People were alarmed by such an act of blasphemy, and none more so than the Mahnheeg, Simeon of Nissim, who recognised a need to protect his people and maintain the sanctity of Jerusalem. Thus, the City Protectors were formed: not as an order of soldiers but one of devoted citizens dedicated to the protection of the city. A call for volunteers was put out, and many men quickly joined, eager to protect the city’s peace. Some enlisted out of recognition over the rising disorder in their community, others with concern for their families and sacred spaces, but all wanted to help. This was their home, and none would defile it.

Armed with swords in a city where no others are permitted to carry weapons, it did not matter that City Protectors had no formal military training — indeed, many had little to no experience with weaponry at all. Those who were once quick to thieve or harm were suddenly more cautious, fearful of the groups of armed men that began to roam the city in organised patterns. People quickly grew to respect the patrols, seeing them as an authority that granted a sense of security even to those less fortunate citizens.

Nonetheless, the newfound presence of the City Protectors has begun to spark some debate, both in Jerusalem and the other Phoenician cities with no such order to speak of. While some see them as a model to be emulated, others consider them a potential threat that could be easily corrupted or used as a force for tyranny. As such, the City Protectors operate with a strict code of ethics rooted in their religious devotion. Their swords are to be seen as symbols of authority rather than instruments of violence, and they work with a deep-rooted conviction that their duty is to protect, not to harm. Still, such conviction and faith in the importance of their work leads to an unyielding sense of entitled justice and some citizens of Jersualem feel that their new champions of safety are a little too big for their own boots and should be restrained in their quest for peace. History
The Cypress Sisterhood was founded in 1090 BC by a healer's wife in the land of Colchis. For many nights, this woman - known later as Artemisa - had dreams of Artemis's most sacred ceryneian hind. In her visions, the creature was casting benevolent kisses upon the pregnant bellies of women. Kisses that seemed to offer protection and good fortune. Artemisa took her dreams as a message from the virgin goddess herself. Already knowledgeable in her husband's trade of herbs and medicinal poultices, Artemisa passed her knowledge to her two blooded sisters and her adult daughter. Together, the four women set out to offer medical aid to the women suffering the most vulnerable time in their lives: pregnancy and childbirth. Initially, the women worked alongside their husbands and partners but, as the order grew, this would eventualy become impossible.

Whilst prenatal and labour care was the first calling of these women who would one day become the Cypress Sisterhood, it soon became apparent that four women living and eating with the small and poor families they intended to help was not the aid they wished to give but an additional burden on already strained resources. They simply solved one issue - the lack of desperately sought medical support - by creating another. The solution to this as found in Artemis's wild and powerful side: that of the huntress. Taking up the bow, the founding women of the sisterhood learned to hunt and to defend oneself whilst camping in the wilds of the forests and fields. Now able to hunt and cook their own food or sell hunted animal hide for coin, the women could offer only goodness to those who sought them.

Women started to join the travelling band. Some were mothers who had lost their children and sought comfort in a good purpose. Others were servants or freed slaves looking for a better life. Some women came to the sisters in order to learn from them; their skills in midwifery and medical practices had become famous around the Colchian isles and across the Aegean. With larger numbers, the sisterhood were able to spread their help and influence further; sending small groups or pairs of women on missions across the provinces. To avoid wild abandon in these women, newly freed from the shackles of their grief, husbands or restrictions, organisation was required. Bylaws and structure within the order. Not to mention a homestead - a large farmhouse - where travelling priestesses could return to. The order, its hierarchy and its home were soon established with Artemisa as the first leader of the group. Training systems were introduced and the doors of the farmhouse were opened wide to those who sought sanctuary. It was this decision that would see Artemisa's husband and others like him falling to the wayside in favour of the patients...

News travelled fast: of a large number of women safely collected within the peaceful woodlands of Illytia. Seeking safety in numbers, amongst Artemis's trees, many who came to the order were not seeking lessons but sanctuary. Women with abusive partners or who had suffered violence at the hands of men were drawn to the beacon of feminity, guarded by Artemis herself. As more battered women arrived, the men who had supported the order were slowly asked to leave. The concept of chastity and its inclusion in the bylaws was discussed and openly agreed upon. Many, surprisingly, believed that such as law should have been instigated from the start, owing to Artemis's status as a virgin deity. With vows of purity made and new himations of white adorned, this collective of women became a formal religious order.

Almost forty years later, war was waged to the south of Colchis by invading Athenian forces and the Crown Prince of Colchis was mortally wounded on the battlefield. A travelling party of the sisterhood were able to help, first in lending their bows to turn the tide of battle then in their skills as physicians, saving the life of the king's only son. In recognition of this great act, King Hector of Kotas ordered a new temple to be built on the site of the sisterhood's farmhouse, in the foothills of Illytia. The sisterhood has kept a strong connection with the Kotas house ever since and The White Temple has forevermore been the centre of their operations: the home of their initiate training, a sanctuary for women fearful of their partners or fathers, a hospital of sorts of the wounded and a place of joy for families reunited by searching priestesses. History
For every civilisation since time immemorial, the prospect of an afterlife has governed the way lives have been lived and softened the sharp inevitability of death. The land of K'm't is no exception. Thus, the Hekasenkt was established centuries ago to bring order from chaos. Certainly not the first death cult in these ancient lands, they stand upon the shoulders of those who have come before, shaping older customs to fit their credence and rising to become the authoritative voice in all things death. Whilst the prospect of a good Duat has always been important in kemetic culture, the Hekasenkt are credited in making it one of the most important aspects of life. After all, mortal life is temporary compared to eternal slumber; thus one's time in the mortal realm should be spent in reverence to the gods and in preparation for the real eternity that awaits.

The Hekasenkt's credence is now so thoroughly ingrained in society but it first enlightened the mind of Pharaoh Aahotepre in 1129 BC. It is written that the Pharaoh, a sickly creature before he was twenty years of age, and the first Priest of Osiris spoke of the Gods and the afterlife whilst locked in the Pharaoh's chamber for seven days and nights. After which, the Pharaoh embarked upon a grand building scheme, including a new temple to the God of Osiris in Thebes built specifically as the home of the Hekasenkt. Pharaoh Aahotepre remained close with the man he assigned as the first Priest of Osiris Meninapta until he died, beginning the tradition of the Hekasenkt caring for the mortuary practices of the monarch.

Over the course of Aahotepre's reign, the Hekasenkt only grew in influence and it was not long before a second in command was appointed to manage the growing daily responsibilities of so large a religious order. Meninapta of Thebes was then free to explore the ethereal elements of the order's worship, such as attempting to communicate with the Gods and viewing the world of Duat through mystical trances.

Though it is uncertain when the first trial of the Teka'Her was practised, rumour tells of it having occurred accidentally when an embalmer was bitten by a snake and believed to be dead. He was set aside for funerary rites before awakening days later when a practitioner made the first incision of mummification. Visions that the embalmer recalled from the other side soon sparked the belief that the door to Duat could be traversed both ways... That death was not merely something to obey, but something that could be conquered. The intensely private and often mute Teka'Hers appeared soon after, shrouding themselves in crimson robes and secret whispers. For secrecy is key. Were the practices they soon developed known to the world, accusations of sacrilege would abound. For who has the right to conquer death, besides the Gods themselves?

Over recent years the Teka-Hers have become more prominent figures within the Hekasenkt, especially with the ascension of Kaemekhem, as the Priest of Anubis. Yet their practises still remain relatively secretive; their supposed communions with the dead are known, as is their ‘vision’ of a new world order, where life triumphs over death. However, rumours continue to swirl of darker deeds: necromancy, dark spells and curses, bodily desecration and even murder. Nothing but vicious rumours, Kaemekhem would tell you, but can one truly trust someone who has claimed to conquer death itself? Roles and Titles

The private ceremony of the Procession of Anubis is far more nefarious and happens away from the public gaze. Most of the Hekasenkt themselves are not privy to what occurs behind closed doors. Though shrouded in mystery, the essence of the Teka'Her is simple: to become "enlightened", which a person can only achieve through death and resurrection. All those who have successfully completed the ritual are believed to have been resurrected, judged pure of heart by the gods and returned to their mortal souls in order to continue in their mission. Those participated in the trial achieve this "death" by ingesting some form of poison, which slows the heart rate so effectively that they actually appear to be dead. They are then wrapped in linens and placed in an open sarcophagus, where they are kept for seven days and nights. Many do actually die during the trial, as the drug can be fatal. These are the souls deemed to have been judged unworthy. Those that survive enter into the inner circle of the Hekasenkt as a Teka'Her; an enlightened master of death.

During this trance, the bereaved is invited to ask questions and the answers they receive are believed to be from their dearly departed. To become part of the Hekasenkt, one is either selected or indoctrinated at an early age. With the importance of funerary practices, the need for embalmers is ever increasing. To address this, the Hekasenkt have established a school within their temple grounds in Thebes and actively recruit for men and women to undertake the training. Of the 20 or so students who begin the training, a quarter will be dismissed before completion and of those who remain only a handful will be selected to join. Others are destined to work as embalmers in smaller temples or for other services.

Indoctrination is perhaps a less common mode for recruitment, but the indoctrinated usually achieve the highest positions within the Hekasenkt. Whilst viewed as a show of benevolence by the masses, the Hekasenkt have a history of taking in orphaned children, giving them shelter, food and an education. Yet, it serves a darker modus operandi. After the unification of the Roma kingdoms, the first monarch to rule all took the name of Imperia. It was a turbulent time, for not all were yet sold on the concept of one king to rule them all. Legends tell that Nintinugga herself selected the first Monarch: a young girl chosen from among the many who prayed at her temple. Over the centuries, the order has grown both in power and in prestige, becoming an essential part of Assyria’s spiritual and medical practices alike. The Monarchs are known not only for their healing abilities but for an unyielding adherence to the strict codes established by their founder and passed down through intense research and traditions.

According to the stories shared throughout Assyria, there was a time when Nintinugga herself walked among mortals, observing the devotion of those who desired her wisdom. One girl stood above the others, showing extraordinary faith and potential far beyond her years. Though the child was of humble origins, the goddess filled her with the divine knowledge that many sought. She was bestowed with the responsibility of founding an order that would guide the worship of Nintinugga for centuries to come and, in honour of this great duty, granted the title of oracle — a title that would one day come to garner the highest respect within the order.

The girl — her name lost to time — grew into an impressive healer, capable of bringing back those who had reached the brink of death in an act of judgement that mirrored divine abilities. In time, she began to establish the rules and the rituals that would govern the worship of Nintinugga, ensuring that the goddess' will was carried out with due precision and reverence. These early teachings continued to emphasise the dual nature of Nintinugga’s power: the ability to heal and restore, but, equally, the authority to judge and decide the fate of souls.

As the first oracle aged, her influence only grew with her, attracting followers drawn to both her wisdom and the divine connection that she embodied. These followers formed the core of what would, in time, become the Monarchs: a dedicated order committed to preserving and expanding upon the teachings of their founder. And yet, as the order evolved in both its members and methods, it continued to follow the set of principles created by the first oracle, ensuring that it remained ever true to Nintinugga’s will.

Since their founding, the Monarchs have only grown in influence. Today, the Monarchs are known not only for their healing abilities but also for their disciplined, almost austere approach to their duties. The legacy of the first Oracle, the child chosen by Nintinugga herself, lives on in every member of the order, guiding their actions and ensuring that they remain faithful to the goddess's divine will. The origins of the Sekrum Dumu trace back to a time of turmoil and an encroachment on sacred Babylonian land. The order was founded by Enki-Zu, who witnessed the unchecked intrusion of foreigners onto the hallowed grounds of Uruk. This contact is subtle and indirect, ensuring that the candidate’s interest is piqued without revealing too much about the faction’s existence. If the candidate responds positively, it is gradually revealed that they are being scouted by the Sekrum Dumu, emphasising the honour and importance of their potential role.

Throughout this process, the candidate is closely monitored. Any signs of reluctance, disloyalty or potential threat to the faction's secrecy are carefully noted. Only when the candidate has demonstrated unwavering commitment and passed a test of loyalty are they invited to undergo the initiation ritual, marking their official entry into the Sekrum Dumu. A travelling circus of exotic spectacle and daring feats, one must be within its inner circle to know the chaotic underbelly of this seedy nomadic cult. To the public, the Tempest is an entertainment troupe of performers, animal tamers, acrobats and illusionists. It is K'm't's grandest and most sought attraction. Sometimes, they perform under a large tent to a big, common-born audience. At others, their services are hired for private gatherings in the homes of the rich and famous, specific talents chosen for particular clientele.

Behind the curtain, these performers are not all created equal. Some are employed for their skills as in any other profession - simple. But the most 'loyal' are brought into the secret fold. The true calling of Set's Tempest is not spectacle but lunacy. Their name is not a fantastical moniker but a confession of their actual devotion. Worshippers of Set and his love of chaos and mania, the Tempest enjoy manipulating audiences and their coin purses. Wherever they travel, theft and street violence increase. After servicing the upper circles, gossip and political allegiances become testy. The Tempest enjoy spreading subtle but powerful discontent whilst their shiny living spectacles dazzle and delight. And the ringmaster ensures efficacy in this dual-purpose by combining emotional manipulation, social control and opiate indoctrination. Standards of Practice
InfluenceThe circus has no official influence upon anyone. But their talents of manipulation has them making waves wherever they go. Currently, this is limited to K'm't and whilst their reputation may reach other lands, they do not currently travel beyond the kemetic borders.

ReligionThe Tempest is greatly devoted to Set and his love of all things chaotic. Their main (but secret) purpose is to breed as much mania as possible without risking their operation. This sinister ambition has been spread like poison wherever they go.

History
Founded by Amenhotep of Momborah, the Tempest was originally created as a commercial venture. Upon witnessing street performers struggling to collect coin on the streets of Memphis, Amenhotep had a concept that bloomed into a grand ambition. Collecting the talented yet vulnerable - particularly women and young children with potential - Amenhotep secured them within his ownership not through slavery but through necessity. He provided them shelter and food. He controlled their lives and how they cultivated their talents. Within a few years, he had a viable, living spectacle on his hands. This included an acrobat - Leda - who would later become his wife.

As the circus's popularity grew, becoming a small, local sensation, so too did Amenhotep's family. His wife produced a son - Somgi - who became Amenhotep's side project within the Tempest. He raised his child with the same vice-like control he exerted over his troupe. During Somgi's childhood, the troupe grew large enough to become mildly self-sufficient and nomadic. Performers were promoted to create a tier of middle management and Amenhotep's ego rose to the level of tyrant. Under Amenhotep's guidance, Somgi learned to be focused, money-hungry and cruel. Upon his death, he learned to be powerful.

That power and control has only grown over the years and Somgi has adapted new methods and techniques for retaining it. Not only has he managed the Tempest into the most sought entertainment troupe in all of K'm't, he has ensured its future by chaining his talents to the big top. A combination of mental abuse, coercion and covert opiate indoctrination, has ensured that Somgi is the epicentre of the Tempest. He is the social core, the tyrannical authority and the source of drugs for his tightest-knit members. It is this inner core that form the cult at the heart of Set's Tempest.

The ugly shadow of tyrannical control is the violent need for influence and a touch of rebellion-seeking. To that end, Somgi leads his cult as experts in chaos. Whilst their actions are never to damage the faction itself, spreading rumours of sedition, causing chaos in the streets and seducing the rich and powerful are all a part of the Tempest's worldview. In the name of Set, they work to snip the puppet-strings of society and needle any form of peace into discomfort and discontent. Roles and Titles

Joining the Order
Recruitment for the troupe can work in either direction. A talented performer might be spotted by an overseer or the troupe's ringmaster and approached for an exclusive contract with the Tempest. Alternatively, entertainers can approach the troupe and seek work with them. It's down to the overseers and ringmaster if they are taken on. All employment contracts begin with a probationary period.
